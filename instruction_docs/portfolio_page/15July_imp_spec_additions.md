A1. FOUR-BOOK ATTRIBUTION RE-RUN (new; runs on the axiom-compliant 1C base, N=60 and N=120; sequence it after P1 if you're mid-flight there). Build one trade ledger — identical entries, exits, prices (the 1C output). Apply four sizing vectors to that same ledger: (1) BASE — equal weight 1/N, fully deployed, no overlays; (2) BASE+SSI — same trades, but on any day the deployment ceiling is below 100%, every position is scaled down uniformly to ceiling × NAV/N and the freed capital sits in cash earning the cash yield — equities scale down, cash scales up, nothing else changes; (3) BASE+CONVICTION — Conviction Engine tiered sizing on single-name equities at full deployment. Hard-blocked equities remain as rows in the trade ledger with their entry and exit dates, but carry $0 in this book. All four books share the exact same list of trades — only the dollars assigned to each trade differ. If the books held different trade lists, return differences could not be attributed purely to sizing (4) ENHANCED — both overlays. Compute each book's Net Asset Value from position values (Axiom 1), daily. Report the decomposition: SSI effect = (2)−(1); Conviction effect = (3)−(1). The two single-overlay effects will not sum perfectly to the ENHANCED return, because the overlays multiply rather than add — a position at 75% conviction size inside an 80% ceiling holds 0.75 × 0.80 of a full slot, not the two effects stacked. Report the small leftover from this multiplication as its own "interaction" row: interaction = (4)−(1)−SSI−Conviction. After the interaction row, the table must sum to the ENHANCED return exactly; anything still left over is a residual, and a residual means the books diverged on something other than sizing — trade membership, prices, or dates — a bug, and the decomposition table tells us where.This is Axiom 0's waterfall applied to the overlays. Why: SSI and Conviction currently live fused inside one NAV; nobody can say what either is worth. Figures like "SSI −0.31%" mean the cumulative profit-and-loss effect of the SSI deployment ceiling on portfolio return — NOT the SSI index reading, which is a separate absolute number. All demo figures are illustrative until this re-run produces real ones. D1. SIZING ENGINE — RETIREMENT INTO THE PORTFOLIO METHOD. The portfolio method becomes the only producer of a signal's size: size = NAV/N × Conviction multiplier (single-name equities only) × SSI ceiling scalar, with sleeve caps enforced as admission slots. Worked example: $10,000,000 portfolio, N=60 → every position $166,667 = 1.67% of NAV. If 12 holdings are US Tech, the sleeve weight = 20% — a true portfolio weight, because 60 slots sum to exactly 100%. US Tech ceiling 12% → maximum 7 slots; the 8th US Tech signal is not resized and not blocked-to-zero — it waits for a US Tech slot to free. Compare the current engine: 61 signals each independently granted 3–10% of a cluster budget → the bar prints 351% and means nothing. Under the new method the bar cannot exceed 100% and breach mathematics runs on real weights (18% + 12% = 30% vs a 20% pair cap is actionable; 357% + 297% = 654% was structural noise).
