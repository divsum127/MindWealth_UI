[29/06/26, 5:18:47 AM] Rohit Sir: How long/short funds work with $100:
Two conventions exist:
Gross exposure basis: $70 long + $30 short = $100 gross exposure. This is "fully invested" in the sense that total capital at risk = $100. Net exposure = $70 - $30 = $40 long. No borrowed money. This is the conservative interpretation and what you want.
Notional basis: $140 long + $40 short = $180 gross, funded by $100 equity + $80 borrowed. This IS leverage — you borrowed $80 to run a larger book. MW runs this way (228% gross). You do not want this.
For MindWealth: $100 gross total, split between longs and shorts. Long notional + short notional = $100 at all times. Net exposure floats between fully long and fully short depending on signal mix. No borrowed money, no leverage.

[29/06/26, 4:42:13 PM] Rohit Sir: MindWealth Layer 1 — Portfolio Construction Process

Starting assumptions:

Total capital: $100
Target portfolio size: N assets (e.g. 60)
Each asset always gets $100/N regardless of direction
Long and short positions treated identically in sizing
No leverage — no borrowed money beyond normal short mechanics
Universe: gated signals only (>60% FWD WR combos)


PHASE 1 — BUILD

Portfolio starts empty. $100 sits in cash.
As signals fire from the gated universe, admit each new asset chronologically.
Each new asset entered gets $100/N allocation from cash.
Remaining cash reduces by $100/N with each new entry.
Continue until N assets held — portfolio fully deployed.
During build: gross exposure = number of assets held × $100/N. Net exposure = (longs − shorts) × $100/N. Both grow toward $100 as portfolio fills.


PHASE 2 — STEADY STATE
Portfolio holds exactly N assets at all times once fully built. Each position = $100/N. No rebalancing of existing positions — they keep their original allocation until they exit naturally.

REPLACING A LONG EXIT

Long signal exits as per exit rule → position closes → $100/N cash freed.
Scan all currently available gated signals not already in portfolio.
Rank by signal quality score (C1–C4 composite).
Admit highest scoring signal — long or short, whichever scores highest.
Deploy $100/N into new position.
Net exposure shifts slightly depending on whether replacement is long or short.
If no signal available → hold cash temporarily until next signal fires.


REPLACING A SHORT EXIT

Short position exits → close position → return borrowed stock → receive back cash collateral → $100/N freed.
Same process as long replacement — scan available signals, take highest quality score.
If replacement is long → net exposure increases slightly (more net long).
If replacement is short → net exposure unchanged directionally.
If no signal available → hold cash, gross exposure temporarily below $100.


NET AND GROSS EXPOSURE — LIVE TRACKING
At any point in time:

Gross exposure = (number of long positions + number of short positions) × $100/N — tells you total capital at risk
Net exposure = (number of long positions − number of short positions) × $100/N — tells you directional bias
Cash = $100 minus gross exposure — only exists during build phase or brief gaps between exit and replacement

Given current signal mix (~90% long, ~10% short), expected steady state:

Gross ≈ $100
Net ≈ +$80 long
This is a net long systematic fund, not market neutral — must be stated explicitly in all presentations


WHAT HAPPENS IF SAME ASSET GETS A NEW SIGNAL WHILE ALREADY HELD:

Already held long, new long signal fires → ignore, position already open
Already held long, new short signal fires → EXIT the long, treat as exit signal, free the slot, replace as per replacement process above
Already held short, new short signal fires → ignore, position already open
Already held short, new long signal fires → EXIT the short, free the slot, replace as per replacement process above


WHAT THIS ACHIEVES:

Equal dollar investment per asset at all times ✓
No leverage — gross never exceeds $100 of own capital ✓
Quality score drives replacements — best available signal always fills vacant slot ✓
Net long bias reflects signal engine's natural output honestly ✓
Simple enough to implement, audit, and explain to an institutional allocator ✓

Outstanding Signals and All Signal Report (website and email) show confirmed and unconfirmed weekly signals. A weekly signal like QQQ on 25 June"will be confirmed at the end of this week"  belongs there. That’s why you saw it on the website Outstanding page.

2. Does the cancellation column mean we wanted unconfirmed early-warning signals?

Yes
Cancellation applies mainly while a signal is still unconfirmed. It states what would invalidate the setup before the interval closes, eg for a long, price must stay above a level through week-end, or the signal is cancelled and never confirms.

Once confirmed, that field becomes "Already confirmed" (or similar), the pre-confirmation rule no longer applies.

So the cancellation column exists because the system surfaces unconfirmed early warnings, not only confirmed trades. It tells you the risk window and invalidation level before you treat the signal as fully valid.
